Know the exam mechanics

The current IREB examination regulations v5.6.2 specify approximately 45 questions in 75 minutes, weighted 1–3 points each. Passing requires at least 70% of the available points. This is not necessarily 70% of the questions.

  • A: choose one answer from four or five alternatives. Watch for negative wording.
  • P: select the specified number. Too many selections yield zero for that question; otherwise correct selections earn proportional credit.
  • K: classify each statement as true or false; each correct classification earns its share.

Aids are prohibited except a language dictionary. For a non-native exam language, a justified request can extend 75 minutes to 90; the certification body must validate it. Check your actual booking and approved duration.

Weekly reviews also include ordering and self-assessed written answers to deepen learning. Those are not official CPRE question formats. Their 100-point scores and Swiss learning grades do not predict certification results.

Printable practice paper

IREB English practice exam v3.3.1, hosted by iSQI: 45 questions, 70 points; 49 points to pass this paper. The document refers to separate answers and a correction aid; those are not included here. Use the interactive resource for feedback if you cannot obtain them.

Take a first timed attempt before memorising solutions. Record why a distractor tempted you. On repeat attempts, familiarity with a paper can inflate your score; explain the rule in a fresh example as well.

What to be able to do

These priorities are a study recommendation based on the course activities and handbook, not predicted exam frequencies.

Distinguish nearby concepts

Requirement versus its written representation; functional requirement, quality requirement and constraint; system boundary versus context boundary versus scope; requirement source versus elicitation technique; verification versus validation. Explain why each rejected alternative is wrong.

Apply principles to a case

Use all nine principles. Identify whose value matters, where shared understanding is missing, which context assumption could fail, and how feedback or change affects the decision. Avoid treating one principle as a universal prescription.

Read and improve work products

Select a suitable level of detail and lifetime. Detect ambiguity and missing acceptance criteria. Read multiplicities, control flow and state transitions. Choose a glossary, template, prototype or model for a stated purpose, and review both individual requirements and the whole set.

Choose and justify an elaboration technique

Identify missing stakeholders and other sources. Relate interviews, observation, collaboration and idea generation to the information gap. Explain Kano categories, diagnose conflicts, and select a validation method suited to the risk.

Close the remaining units 5–7

Compare process choices and tailoring. Study requirements attributes, views, prioritisation, traceability, versioning, baselines and change management. Explain what tools support and how to select and introduce them. Use the syllabus to check the complete objective list; this paragraph is only a starting checklist.
